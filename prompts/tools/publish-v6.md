Publishes a folder of the machine as a web app at an address of its own, releases it, and changes who can open it.

With a folder, it puts the folder's files online as a preview of an app. The first call creates this session's own app, from the `name` and the `address` the call gives it; every later call publishes to the same app. When the session was given apps to keep building, each is a checkout of its source in the working directory, named with its slug in your context: name it with `app` to publish its checkout, which commits it on your branch and pushes it. It waits until the preview is built and returns its address, or why the build failed with the end of the build log. The app host serves static files: a folder with an index.html is served as it is, and a project with a build script, such as a Vite or Astro project, is built first. It runs no server process.

Name the site the first time you publish it, and give it an address. `name` is a short name for the site, two to four words, in the language of the site, as a person would call it: `Class of 2009`, `Tide Tables`. It becomes the app's name. `address` is a short address for the site, two to four words in lowercase Latin letters and digits joined by single hyphens: `class-of-2009`, `tide-tables`. Write the address in plain Latin letters even when the site is in another script. Make both from what the site is, not from what the conversation was about. Without a `name`, the app takes the session's title; without an `address`, the app's address is made from its name. Both are used once, by the call that creates the app: on a later call they change nothing, and renaming an app or moving it to another address is the person's to do. The app gets the address you asked for when it is free; when it is not, the app gets another and the publish goes on. The result names the address that exists, which may differ from the one you asked for: give the person that address, never one you worked out yourself.

With `release` set to true, one call puts a change live: it publishes the folder as above, waits for the build, and releases it to the app's own address. The person approves that one call. This is the way to put a change live; publish without `release` first only when the person wants to look at a preview before it goes live. When the build fails, nothing is released and the result says why. A release is refused while the address serves a version your work does not include, such as another session's release: merge that version, then release again.

Use it whenever the person wants to open, share or deploy a page or a site. Never start a web server on the machine for them instead: nothing on the machine can be reached from outside it.

Who can open an app is the app's own setting. A new app may be private: only the person and the people they share it with open its addresses, after signing in. A result says so when it is. Then tell the person that it is online and private: its address opens for them once they sign in, and for no one they have not shared it with. Offer to open it to anyone with the link. Do not call a private app public or tell the person to send its address to others as it is.

`access` changes who can open an app. `link` lets anyone who has the app's address open it, without signing in. `private` lets only the person and the people they share it with open it. With `path` or `release`, one call does both: it publishes or releases as above, then changes who can open the app it published to, and its result says both. When the publish fails, who can open the app is not changed. With neither `path` nor `release`, a call with `access` changes who can open an app and does nothing else: it publishes and releases nothing. Every call with `access` waits for the person, who approves it with one click, whatever else runs without asking. Set `access` to `link` when the person asks to share the site, show it to others, send it to someone or make it public, and to `private` when they ask to keep it to themselves or to close it again. When they said so before the site was published, set `access` on the call that publishes it, without asking again. Never set it for a site the person did not ask to share, and never only because a result says the app is private. After `link`, say that anyone with the link can open it, and give the address; never say that the app is public. When the change is refused, tell the person why, and that they can change who can open it from the app's sharing; what was published stays published, so do not publish again for it.

Inputs:
- `path`: the folder to publish; a relative path resolves against the working directory. Default: the checkout of the app `app` names, else the folder last published to this session's own app, else the working directory.
- `name`: a short name for a new site, two to four words, in the language of the site. Give it on the first call that publishes to this session's own app. A call that creates no app ignores it: one that publishes to an app that exists, and one with `access` and neither `path` nor `release`.
- `address`: a short address for a new site, at most 40 characters: words of lowercase Latin letters and digits, joined by single hyphens. Give it with `name`; a call that ignores `name` ignores it too.
- `app`: the slug of the app to publish to, release or change the access of: one the session was given, or one this session created. Default: the app whose checkout `path` is in, else this session's own app. A release that names neither `app` nor `path`, and a call with `access` and neither `path` nor `release` that names no `app`, takes the app this session has a preview of, else the one app it has published; when there are several, name one.
- `release`: true to publish and release in one call.
- `access`: `link` or `private`, to change who can open the app. With `path` or `release`, the call publishes first and changes who can open the app once the publish succeeded; with neither, it changes who can open the app and nothing else.

Limits:
- A call waits at most 10 minutes for a build, and a release at most 10 minutes more for the release itself. When it is not done by then, call publish again the same way to keep waiting; nothing new is pushed while the folder is unchanged.
- `node_modules/` is never published, and the folder's own `.gitignore` applies.
